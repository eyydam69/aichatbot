NATIONAL DISABILITY INSURANCE SCHEME (NDIS) - OVERVIEW

What is the NDIS?
The National Disability Insurance Scheme (NDIS) is an Australian government program that provides support to people with permanent and significant disability, their families, and carers. The NDIS was launched in 2013 and began full rollout across Australia in 2016.

Key Objectives:
1. Provide individualised support to people with disability
2. Improve outcomes for people with disability
3. Build community awareness and inclusion
4. Provide certainty and security for families
5. Give people with disability choice and control over their lives

Who is Eligible?
To access the NDIS, a person must:
- Be under 65 years of age when they first enter the scheme
- Be an Australian citizen or hold a permanent visa or protected special category visa
- Have a permanent disability that significantly affects their ability to take part in everyday activities
- Live in an area where the NDIS is available

What Does the NDIS Fund?
The NDIS funds reasonable and necessary supports that help participants to:
- Pursue their goals and aspirations
- Increase their independence
- Develop skills for daily living
- Participate in the community
- Find and keep employment
- Improve their health and wellbeing

Types of Supports:
1. Core Supports - everyday activities and needs
2. Capital Supports - investments like equipment and home modifications
3. Capacity Building Supports - skill development and independence

NDIS Plan Management:
Participants can choose how their plan is managed:
- NDIS Managed: NDIS pays providers directly
- Plan Managed: A plan manager handles payments
- Self Managed: Participant manages their own funds

Average Funding:
The average annual NDIS plan is approximately $70,000 per participant, though this varies significantly based on individual needs. Some participants may receive much higher funding for complex support needs.

Review Process:
NDIS plans are typically reviewed annually. Participants can request a review if their circumstances change. The plan review process includes:
- Assessing current supports
- Evaluating goal progress
- Adjusting funding levels
- Planning for the next period

Key Statistics (2024):
- Over 600,000 participants currently supported
- Annual budget exceeding $40 billion
- Over 270,000 workers providing NDIS services
- Average wait time for access decisions: 40 days

Complaints and Appeals:
If participants are unhappy with NDIS decisions, they can:
1. Request an internal review by the NDIA
2. Apply to the Administrative Appeals Tribunal (AAT)
3. Contact the Commonwealth Ombudsman
4. Seek support from disability advocacy organizations
